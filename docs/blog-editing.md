# Writing for the Acceleret blog

Everything happens at **https://acceleret.com/admin**. Sign in with the email and password an
admin gave you. Nothing here needs code or a redeploy: publish, and the article is on the site
the next time someone opens the page.

## Write an article

1. Go to **Posts › Create new**.
2. **Title**: what the article answers, in plain words ("How to move stock tracking off Excel").
3. **Excerpt**: one or two sentences shown on the blog list. Google uses it too if you leave the
   SEO description empty.
4. **Cover image** (optional): a wide picture, about 1600 × 900.
5. **Content**: write the article (see below).
6. In the right-hand column, pick a **Category**, add **Tags** (press Enter after each) and check
   the **Author**.
7. Open the **SEO** tab (see below).
8. **Publish changes** puts it live. Until then it is a draft that only signed-in people can see.
   Your work saves itself every few seconds while you type.

The web address comes from the title (`/blog/how-to-move-stock-tracking-off-excel`). You can edit
it in the **Slug** field before publishing. Avoid changing it after, because links to the old
address will stop working.

## The editor

Select text to get the formatting bar: bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, inline code, link.
The toolbar at the top, and typing **/** on an empty line, give you everything else:

| What | How to use it |
| --- | --- |
| **Headings** | H2 for each main section, H3 for parts inside it, H4 for small labels. Don't skip levels. The title is already the page's H1. The H2s and H3s become the "In this article" list. |
| **Lists** | Bulleted, numbered, or checklist. |
| **Links** | Select text, then click the link button. Choose **Internal link** to point to another article. For other sites, paste the full address. Tick *Open in new tab* for other sites, and *nofollow* for paid, sponsored or untrusted links. |
| **Images** | **/upload** or the image button, or drag a picture file into the article, or paste one. Every image needs **alt text**: one sentence describing it for people who can't see it. Google reads it too. Add a **caption** with the pencil button on the image if the picture needs explaining. |
| **Moving things** | Point at an image (or a paragraph, table or box) and a **⋮⋮** grip appears to its left. Drag the grip up or down; a blue line shows where it will land. Dragging the picture itself does nothing. |
| **Tables** | **/table**. Keep the first row as the header. Wide tables scroll sideways on phones by themselves. |
| **Quote** | A line worth pulling out. It shows in handwriting. |
| **Callout** | One point readers must not miss: a note, tip or warning. |
| **FAQ** | Questions and short answers. Google may show them under the article in search results. |
| **Call to action** | The blue box with a button. The default sends readers to the walkthrough booking page. |
| **Divider** | A dotted line between sections. |

Bold appears on the site as a soft highlight rather than heavy type, to match the site's style.

An image always sits between paragraphs, never inside one: dropped in the middle of a sentence, it
goes after that paragraph. To put it between two sentences, press Enter there first. Small images
show at their own size, and the align buttons can place them left, centre or right. Large images
always fill the column.

## The SEO tab

- **Meta title**: what shows as the blue link on Google. Around 50–60 characters. *Auto-generate*
  fills it from the title.
- **Meta description**: the grey text under the link. About 150 characters, written to make
  someone click. *Auto-generate* copies the excerpt.
- **Meta image**: the picture shown when the article is shared on WhatsApp, LinkedIn and so on.
  Without one, the cover image is used. Without either, a card with the title is drawn
  automatically.
- The **preview** shows roughly how the result will look on Google.
- **Canonical URL**: leave empty, unless the article first appeared on another website. Then paste
  that address here.
- **Hide from search engines**: for articles that shouldn't appear on Google. They stay on the blog,
  but are left out of the sitemap and the RSS feed.

## Good habits for search

- One topic per article, answering a question people actually type into Google.
- Put the main phrase in the title, the first paragraph and at least one H2.
- Link to one or two related articles, and to the relevant service page (`/services#crm`, …).
- Name image files sensibly before uploading (`stock-dashboard.png`, not `IMG_2041.png`).
- Updating an old article is often worth more than writing a new one. The "Updated" date shows
  on the page and tells Google it is fresh.

## Categories, images and people

- **Categories**: each has its own page (`/blog/category/<name>`). A category appears on the blog
  only once it has a published article.
- **Media**: every uploaded image. Fix alt text here, and it updates everywhere the image is used.
- **Users** (admins only): add a person with their email and a temporary password. If someone
  forgets their password, an admin sets a new one for them here.

## Unpublish or delete

- **Unpublish** (in the menu next to the Publish button) takes the article off the site and keeps
  it as a draft.
- **Delete** removes it for good. Prefer unpublishing.
- Every save is kept under **Versions**, so you can look back or restore an earlier one.
